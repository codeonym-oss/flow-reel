# 005: The master is 1440p (device scale 4/3), x264 ultrafast CRF 10

**Date:** 2026-11-02 (building, measured on the e2e run; agreed: "ok")

**Supersedes 001** (its 4K resolution and CRF ~10 `veryfast` settings; Xvfb + ffmpeg `x11grab` and the constant
30 fps stand).

**Question:** the 4K master can't be encoded in real time while Chromium renders 4K in software: the capture falls
behind and the video ends before the demo does. What should the master be?

**Decision:** **a 2560×1440 master: the CSS viewport stays 1920×1080, drawn at device scale 4/3, captured by
x11grab and encoded with x264 `ultrafast` CRF 10.** The runner fails a recording whose master is more than 3 % shorter
than the wall-clock recording time, so a machine that can't keep up never yields a lagging video silently.

Measured with Chromium scrolling an animated page, AMD Ryzen 5 3500U (4 cores, 8 threads), in the image:

| Master | Encoder | Speed | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4K | x264 ultrafast CRF 10 | 0.63× | 9 MB / 6 s |
| 4K | utvideo (lossless) | 0.79× | 260 MB / 6 s |
| 1440p | x264 ultrafast CRF 10 | 0.99× | 19 MB / 6 s |
| 1440p | x264 veryfast CRF 12 | 0.53× | 5 MB / 6 s |

- **Why:** the only measured setting that keeps up, here and on CI runners of the same size; scripts and
  `events.json` stay in 1920×1080 CSS pixels.
- **Not chosen:** a per-machine master with `--master 4k` now (two configurations to test; kept as a follow-up);
  recording 4K at half speed (the app's own animations and video would play at double speed).
- **Cost accepted:** zooms stay fully sharp up to 1.33×; a 2× zoom (GH-7) upscales by 1.5×.
